Placement Rule

Lattice generated by v1=(3,2) and v2=(−2,5) in axial coordinates — a minimum center-to-center distance of 5 hexagons. Every solid tile on the diagram is a hexagon actually occupied by an HQ (distance ≤1 from its center); every pale tile is an empty hexagon, deliberately left as a buffer because it's still within distance ≤2 of an HQ.

That distance ≤2 is what blocks the enemy: to insert an HQ, they'd need a center at distance ≥3 from every neighboring HQ — this lattice never leaves that much room.

One HQ layer in Grass = a band of 5 hex rings (the lattice's center-to-center spacing). Hence Grass = rings 36–40, 41–45, 46–50, 51–55, 56–60.

The ring is split into 5 equal 72° zones around the center, Zone 1 centered due east, then clockwise. Each zone is one color on the diagram; the table below batches its HQs into squads of 75, ordered from the center outward — anything past 75 turns gray.

Model Limitations

  • 5 hexagons is the absolute minimum gap that still prevents insertion — any closer (distance 3 or 4) and either HQs touch, or an insertable gap reappears.
  • 5 zones don't align with the hexagon's 6 natural corners, so zone boundaries cut across the shape asymmetrically — purely a squad-organization choice, not a geometric one.
  • At the inner/outer edges of the ring, a few buffer tiles spill one hexagon into the Capital zone or beyond the grass — negligible in practice.
Exact calculation — Mud: 150 HQ (2,862 terrain hexes) · Grass 5 HQ layers: 378 HQ (rings 36–60) · minimum gap 5 hex, blocking radius 2 (mathematical optimum) · 5 zones, squads of 75
